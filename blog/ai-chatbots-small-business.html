Are AI chatbots worth it for small businesses?

When a chatbot genuinely helps, when it hurts, and how to set one up so customers actually like it.

Tim DalyApril 20263 min read

AI chatbots are everywhere now, and plenty of businesses are being sold one. Some are genuinely useful. Others annoy customers and quietly lose you enquiries. Here's how to tell whether a chatbot makes sense for your business, and how to set one up so it helps rather than hurts.

What a modern chatbot can actually do

Older chatbots followed rigid scripts and got stuck the moment you asked something unexpected. Today's AI chatbots can understand normal questions and answer from your own information, such as your services, prices, opening hours and FAQs. Done well, they can:

  • Answer common questions at 10pm when you're not at your desk.
  • Collect the details you need for a quote, like suburb, job type and photos.
  • Point people to the right page or booking link.
  • Pass hot leads to you straight away, by email or text.

When a chatbot is worth it

  • You get the same ten questions over and over.
  • Customers contact you outside business hours.
  • You lose enquiries because you can't reply fast enough.
  • Your quotes need the same information every time.

When it isn't

  • You only get a handful of enquiries a week, and you answer them personally.
  • Your work is complex and every job needs a real conversation.
  • You don't have clear information for it to learn from yet.

In those cases, a clear website and a good contact form will do more for you.

How to do it well

  1. Train it on your real information. Your services, areas, prices and policies. Not generic internet knowledge.
  2. Set limits. It should say "I'm not sure, let me get Tim to call you" rather than make something up.
  3. Make it obvious it's AI. Customers don't like being tricked, and honesty builds trust.
  4. Always offer a human. A phone number or "talk to a person" option should be one tap away.
  5. Read the conversations. Check them weekly at first. They'll show you what customers really want to know, which is gold for your website too.

Watch the privacy side

Chatbots collect personal information, so treat it carefully. Know where conversations are stored, don't ask for more than you need, and mention it in your privacy policy.
