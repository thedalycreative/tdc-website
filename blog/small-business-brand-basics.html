What a small business brand actually needs (it’s not just a logo).

You don’t need a 60-page brand book. Six simple things that make a small business look established and trustworthy.

Tim DalyFebruary 20263 min read

When most small business owners hear "brand", they think of a logo. The logo matters, but it's the smallest part. Your brand is what people remember about you and what they say when they recommend you.

The good news is you don't need a big agency or a 60-page brand book. Here's what a small business brand actually needs.

1. A clear sentence about what you do

Before colours and fonts, you need words. Can you explain who you help, what you do, and why you're the right choice, in one plain sentence? For example: "Friendly, reliable pool maintenance for busy families in Perth's northern suburbs."

That sentence will guide your website, your social media and how you answer the phone.

2. A simple logo that works small

Your logo will appear on phone screens, invoices, uniforms and Google listings, often very small. Simple usually wins. Make sure you have versions that work on light and dark backgrounds, and a square version for social media profiles.

3. Two or three colours, used consistently

Pick a main colour, a supporting colour and a neutral. Write down the exact colour codes and use them everywhere. Consistency is what makes a small business look established.

4. One or two fonts

A heading font and a body font is plenty. Choose fonts that are easy to read on a phone, and use the same ones on your website, quotes and signage.

5. A recognisable voice

How do you sound? Friendly and relaxed, or polished and formal? Neither is wrong, but mixing them is confusing. If your website sounds like a law firm and your Facebook sounds like a mate at the pub, customers won't know which one is really you.

6. Real photos

Stock photos of smiling people in headsets don't build trust. Photos of your actual team, your work and your premises do. A few hours with a local photographer is one of the best brand investments you can make.

Put it on one page

Write all of this down on a single page: your sentence, logo files, colour codes, fonts, voice and a few example photos. Share it with anyone who creates anything for your business, from your sign writer to your social media helper. That one page will do more for your brand than another logo redesign.
